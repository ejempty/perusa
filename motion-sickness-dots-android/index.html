Motion sickness dots on Android: which phones have a setting, and what the rest can use

Updated October 25, 2026 (the built-in feature facts were last checked September 19, 2026) · by Elliott Jempty, developer of the Perusa motion sickness app

The "car sick dots" people share on social media are a phone feature that draws small dots at the edges of the screen and moves them with the car. The idea is simple: your inner ear feels the car brake and turn, and the dots let your eyes see that same motion while you read. On iPhone the feature is called Vehicle Motion Cues. On Android it depends on your phone.

Which Android phones have it built in

Google added a built-in version called Motion Assist with Android 17. According to 9to5Google and Android Authority, it reached Pixel phones first and then Samsung Galaxy phones running One UI 9 (Android 17), in September 2026. Phones that stay on Android 16 or older do not get it.

How to check your phone

  1. Open Quick Settings (swipe down twice) and look for a Motion Assist tile. It may be among the unused tiles behind the pencil or Edit button.
  2. Or open Settings and search for "Motion Assist" or "motion sickness".
  3. Still nothing? Settings, then About phone: check the Android version. Below 17 means no Motion Assist. Some phone makers add their own motion sickness features, so a Settings search is worth it either way.

If you have it, use it first: it is free and already on the phone. More on Motion Assist and older phones.

What Android 8 to 16 phones can use

Most Android phones in use today run something older than Android 17, and many will never get it. For those phones, motion cue apps draw the same kind of dots over whatever you are reading. Several are on Google Play, some free; search "motion cues" or "motion sickness" and try one or two. Things worth checking in any of them:

  • Do the dots jump on rough roads? The motion linked to car sickness is slow (about 0.1 to 0.5 Hz, braking and cornering). Dots that bounce with every pothole add movement your body does not feel.
  • Do taps pass through? An overlay that blocks the app underneath is hard to live with.
  • Ads and subscriptions. Ads over a reading app are distracting; check whether features sit behind a subscription.
  • Does it stay out of the way when you're not riding? It should start on its own in a moving car and stand down after the ride.

Questions

Do Samsung phones have motion sickness dots?

Galaxy phones that have received One UI 9 (Android 17) have Motion Assist, according to the September 2026 reports above. Galaxy phones on One UI 8 or older do not; an app is the option there.

Does Android have a car sickness setting?

Android itself has one from Android 17: Motion Assist, on Pixel and recent Galaxy phones. Some phone makers add their own, so search Settings for "motion" or "car sickness". Reducing animations in Accessibility settings is a different thing and does not show the car's motion.

Is there a free motion sickness dots app for Android?

Yes, there are free ones on Google Play. Perusa is free to install with 3 hours of rides before a $1.99 one-time unlock (US price).

Do the dots work on a tablet?

Perusa needs a phone: it reads speed from GPS to know you are riding, and most Wi-Fi tablets have no GPS.
