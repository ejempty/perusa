Can you get Apple's Vehicle Motion Cues on Android?

Updated October 25, 2026 (feature facts last checked September 19, 2026) · by Elliott Jempty, developer of the Perusa motion sickness app

Short answer: not the Apple feature itself, but the same idea, yes. Vehicle Motion Cues is an accessibility feature Apple announced in May 2024 for iPhone and iPad (iOS 18 and later). It draws animated dots at the edges of the screen that move with the vehicle, so your eyes see the motion your body feels. It is part of Apple's operating system, so there is no Android version and no way to install it on an Android phone.

What Android has instead

  • Motion Assist, built into Android 17. Google's own version of the idea reached Pixel phones and Samsung Galaxy phones on One UI 9 in September 2026 (9to5Google, Android Authority). If your phone has Android 17, look for it in Quick Settings or search Settings. How to check, step by step.
  • Motion cue apps, for Android 8 to 16. Most Android phones are not on Android 17 yet, and many never will be. Apps on Google Play draw the same kind of dots over whatever you are reading; some are free. What to look for in one.

Perusa

Perusa is the app I make, for passengers on phones with Android 8 or newer. Like Apple's feature, it draws dots at the edges of the screen that move with the car's real braking, speeding up and turns. Its dots follow only the slow motion of braking and cornering (about 0.1 to 0.5 Hz) and stay still over potholes and road shake, and it works over any app: e-books, your browser, messages, video and games. It is free to install, and after your first 3 hours of rides it is $1.99 once to unlock (US price), with no ads and no subscription. See Perusa on Google Play.

We have not compared Perusa side by side with Vehicle Motion Cues or Motion Assist, so we make no claim about which works better.

Questions

Is there an Android version of Vehicle Motion Cues?

No. Vehicle Motion Cues is built into Apple's operating system. Android's built-in equivalent is Motion Assist on Android 17; on older Android phones, motion cue apps do the same job.

My friend has the car sick dots on her iPhone. What do I search for on Android?

On an Android 17 phone, search Settings for "Motion Assist". On anything older, search Google Play for "motion cues" or "motion sickness dots".

Do the dots help everyone?

No. Many people find reading as a passenger more comfortable with them, and some notice little difference. Sitting in front, fresh air and regular breaks still matter; see how to read in the car without getting sick.
