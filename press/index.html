In about 25 words

Perusa adds motion cue dots over any Android app, moving with the car's braking and turns for passengers. Free to install; 3 hours of rides free, then $1.99 once.

In about 100 words

Perusa is a motion sickness app for car passengers on Android. Small motion cue dots at the edges of the screen move with the car's real braking, speeding up and turns, so a passenger's eyes see the motion their body feels, over any app: e-books, the browser, messages, video, games. The dots follow only the slow motion linked to car sickness and stay still over potholes and road shake. It runs on phones with Android 8 or newer; most Android phones cannot get Android 17's built-in Motion Assist yet. Perusa is free to install: the first 3 hours of rides are free, then a $1.99 one-time unlock. No ads, no account. Made by one independent developer.

What it isA visual comfort aid for car passengers: motion cue dots drawn over other apps that move with the vehicle's braking, acceleration and turns, and hold still over potholes and road shake (it follows the 0.1–0.5 Hz band of ISO 2631-1 and filters out 2–20 Hz)

What we do and don't claim

  • Perusa is a comfort aid, not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition, and it may not help everyone.
  • For passengers only. It asks the rider to confirm they are a passenger, and asks again every 90 days.
  • The dots move with the car's motion; they do not predict it.
  • We have not yet compared Perusa side by side with Apple's Vehicle Motion Cues or Android's Motion Assist, so we make no claim about which works better.
